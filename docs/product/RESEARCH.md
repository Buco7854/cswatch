# Design references — 2026-11-16

## Refrag (observed live)

Sources: https://refrag.gg/ and public https://play.refrag.gg/login . Browser screenshots captured locally under ignored `.design/references/` for research only; not bundled as cswatch assets.

The current public site uses a strongly drawn wordmark, large angular/condensed display type, a dark field, a decisive primary action and a recognizable frame/motion language. The feature narrative is organized around what the player is trying to accomplish, with a product example as evidence. Public navigation and sign-in/product entry are distinct.

The public sign-in page combines a focused form, Steam/email choices, recovery/registration paths and a game-world image. Its branding persists through that transition, while the form itself remains conventional and easy to scan.

Transfer principles: distinctive type and mark; an intentional public-to-product transition; task-based feature explanation; recognizable visual grammar across landing/auth/app; actual product proof. Do not copy Refrag's logo, typography, red/blue palette, illustration, claims, pricing, stats or screenshots into cswatch.

## FACEIT (limited observation)

The official https://www.faceit.com/en page presented Cloudflare verification to the research browser. Its current authenticated UI was not inspected, and no bypass was attempted. The user's reference establishes a desired level of esports identity/craft; it does not establish specific observations about the current FACEIT interface.

## cswatch implications

The rejected designs were tidy shells with a weak product story. A distinct identity must be recognizable in type, mark, frame, rhythm, copy and interactions together. The strongest subject-specific material is replay: returning to a moment, changing perspective, and navigating a round. That should shape the brand and hero's live demonstration, rather than arbitrary decorative polygons or fake personal analytics.
