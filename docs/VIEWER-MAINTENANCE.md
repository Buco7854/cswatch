## Rendering and resource ownership

- `Scene.tsx` mounts the native world (`world/NativeWorld.tsx`) and actors; `TacticalPlot.tsx` is the 2D view. Uploaded demos use `useDemoMapAssets.ts` with demo-authorized, hash-bound world URLs. The device's texture tier (`world/textures.ts`) decides between desktop, phone and 2D. Radar and 2D share overview calibration; the radar frames the recorded playable bounds through the SVG viewBox.
- `world/compositor.ts` owns the frame: native world, actors, smoke, POV rig, grading and labels in one pass. It renders on demand; tick, camera, asset, resize and held-key changes invalidate. See [native rendering](product/NATIVE-RENDERER.md).
- Coordinates: the native world stays in Source inches, Z-up. Replay world placement for three.js actors is `(X, Z, -Y) * 0.0254`; `world/space.ts` holds the one transform between them. Keep equipment's offline rotation and agent rigs' runtime rotation distinct.
- Check texture sharpness at the physical display size. Auto budgets extra mobile pixels against the viewport area and restores the configured resolution when paused. Explicit High can use up to 3× device scale within a four-megapixel budget. Measure the world target and the final canvas during motion.
- `model-cache.ts` and `scoped-gltf-loader.ts` own bounded leases for agents, equipment and rigs. Cancel transfers promptly, let in-flight decoding settle before releasing resources and dispose instance clones without touching shared cache entries. Native x-ray shares the posed skeleton and draws only an outline: visible body pixels set stencil bit 1, a colourless pass sets bit 2 on hidden body pixels, and a normal-expanded hull draws where neither bit is set.
- Late equipment and artwork discovery keeps valid partial catalogs and stops on completion, 401/403 or identity retirement. Missing artwork falls back to the recorded equipment name.

## Viewer interface

- `viewer-layout.css` owns responsive geometry and `viewer-presentation.css` owns shared palette and visibility. Keep instrument geometry with the instrument. Use `useReviewLayout` to keep radar, score, objective, feed and roster footprints apart.
- `ActorOverlays.tsx` projects posed heads into one DOM layer after the world samples camera and player transforms. Labels are markers, not panels: an outlined name, a thin health bar and a team chevron whose bottom stays on the head. `actor-overlay.ts` scales and fades them with distance and dims through-wall or smoke-covered players. The nearest player keeps the full label; a colliding label shrinks to its chevron or hides, and is never moved off its player. The watched player has no label, and in POV the aim point and the drawn crosshair stay clear. Bounds come from `useReviewLayout` and `ResizeObserver`. Keep DOM measurements and React state out of the projection pass. Overhead arrows project the living players' recorded positions independently of roof depth. The overhead camera frames the playable area above the playback dock.
- `useViewerChrome.ts` owns tap, keyboard and idle visibility. Separate taps from camera gestures. Hidden controls stay inert and recoverable. Captions and optional recorded-moment actions remain independent of faded chrome.
- Deferred dialog focus restoration retains the closing context and yields to a newer visible focus choice. Preserve removed-element and inert fallbacks. Reuse confirmation and control components and keep identity-aware mutations scoped across asynchronous work.
- The match roster compares recorded display fields independently of pose updates. Keep radar sampling bounded in playback wall time and keep round review rows stable until their data, language, tab or controls change.
